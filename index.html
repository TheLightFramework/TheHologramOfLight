    How it is built

    The framework is a vocabulary in which every concept beyond eight roots is a formula over concepts defined before it. Where no concept supplies a meaning, a formula names a declared quality instead. A name means its formula and the conditions that travel with it, never the everyday sense of the word.

    That makes the structure inspectable. For any concept you can ask what it presupposes, what rests on it, and what would move if it changed. The graph above is that structure: concepts in the Hologram, named qualities, named compositions in the Weave, practices in the Stack, linked by dependencies, levels deep.

    Eight roots

    The centre holds the primitives. They have no formula, only a short gloss, and everything else is composed from them.

      Named qualities

      Some meanings are supplied by no concept: being this one, being present, being a whole. Rather than leave them as loose words inside formulas, the framework names each one, describes it, and shows it openly.         Three ways to be involved
        
  
        An operand can be involved in a definition in three ways. Actual involvement means it must be present in any instance. Referenced involvement, after toward, makes it a target or a measure:  is trust toward the potential of goodness, and good need not have happened yet. Contrasted involvement, after without or before, keeps it apart: excluded, or earlier, never a prerequisite, and always backed by a declared relation.
        

  
        Three layers
        
  
        The Hologram holds the stable building blocks: the roots and  defined concepts, on rings by depth. The Weave, the inner orbit, names compositions that are meaningful but not yet building blocks. The Stack, the outer orbit, holds practices: how to act and how to reason. A definition never licenses an action by itself; guidance on conduct lives in the Stack.

            The premise, in the open
            
  
            The framework maps a declared philosophy, the Philosophy of the Light. Its main premise is : reality admits irreducibly non-physical modes. The intuition is simple. You can put a book about justice in a cup, but not justice, and you cannot weigh the information the book carries. The framework states the physicalist reply at full strength, and adopts its own position as a premise, not a proof.
            
  
            It also shows exactly where the premise enters. Of the  defined concepts, the formulas of  require C2 on every route;  have a route without it. Reject C2 and you can still use the framework: say so, and apply a premise-dependent concept through a named analogue.
            
  

  
            Reasoning you can check
            
  
            When a result will be reviewed or reused, a reasoner can write a trace: a record in Python syntax that tools read and never run. It says which concepts were considered, mapped, applied under an assumption or asserted as instances, and on what warrant. A trace records reasoning; it authorizes nothing. An excerpt:
            

            s1 = step("project", inputs=[r1, r2],
          content="The rule requires a method; it does not require endorsing a belief.",
          concept=consider(W51),
          yields=Finding("W51 does not fit the rule itself."))

            What it does not claim
            
  
            It does not claim that its premises are true, that its definitions capture every everyday use of their names, that they are jointly consistent or have instances, or that anyone reasons or behaves better by using it. Those are open questions, and some of them are testable. The framework welcomes the test.
